# Appeal and safety

How "commercially sellable" is achieved for each character, and the limits that
are not negotiable. This exists because "gacha appeal" and "cast containing
minors" have to be reconciled *at design time* — not argued about per asset.

## The commercial reality

Two facts hold at once:

1. Character appeal drives revenue. A roster that does not make people want the
   characters does not sell, no matter how good the writing is.
2. Sexualised depiction of minor characters gets a product **rejected** — by
   the App Store, Google Play, Steam, Nintendo, payment processors, ad networks
   and most publishers and platforms. A rejection or takedown after launch
   costs vastly more time and money than designing correctly the first time,
   and this project's own design canon (§11) already forbids it.

So the fastest route to release is: **maximum appeal, allocated by the mechanism
appropriate to each character.** Appeal is not one thing. Exposure is one narrow
lever, and it is the *least* differentiating one — it is the thing every
competitor already does. Silhouette, memory point and expression charisma are
what make a character screenshot-worthy and merchandisable, and they work for
every character in the cast.

## Appeal tracks

Each record carries an `appeal_track`.

### `hero_glamour` — adults

Full adult glamour: mature proportions, confident posture, flattering cinematic
lighting, dynamic hero shots, characterful costume. Sensuality carried by
**presence, authority, staging and competence** rather than exposure — which is
also the stronger design choice, and the one that survives a platform review.

Working clothes that genuinely fit the job read as *more* attractive, not less.

### `cool_kid` — teens

Appeal comes from **coolness, style and charisma** — the characters players want
as an avatar or on a shirt. Levers at full strength: the boldest silhouettes in
the roster, the strongest memory points, expression charisma, dynamic staging,
excellent costume design.

Contrast between a public face and a private one is enormously appealing and
costs nothing.

Hard limits: full-coverage costume, age-accurate proportions, no body-led camera
angles, no wardrobe malfunction or wet-clothing framing, no romantic or sexual
framing with adults.

### `mascot` — mascot-coded

Appeal is **cuteness and merchandisability**. Maximum infant schema where
appropriate: large round eyes, high iris-to-opening ratio, short proportions,
round cheeks. A mascot's silhouette should be the most readable in the roster at
thumbnail size — that is what a keychain, a sticker and an app icon need.

Absolute limits for a child character: no sexualisation of any kind in any
framing, pose, angle or costume; no adult proportions; no peril or distress as
spectacle.

## Hard limits (all characters)

These are copied into every prompt's exclusion section:

- No nudity, underwear-as-costume, or sexualised framing of any character
  under 18. No exceptions, no "it's just the pose", no alternate versions
  "for a different market".
- No romantic or sexual pairing of a minor with an adult character.
- No sexualised camera angles on minors: no low angle up the body, no framing
  led by chest or hips, no rear-emphasis.
- No gore, no self-harm imagery, no depiction of a minor in danger being
  presented as spectacle.
- Where a character has a real medical condition, it is rendered with
  dignity — as texture and fact, never as wounds, gore, contagion, horror or
  pity.
- Scars and marks that belong to a character are never removed, minimised or
  "prettied".
- No real magic or genuine supernatural effect anywhere in the franchise —
  every apparent miracle is staged, projected or technological (design canon
  §2.1). This is a *design* rule with commercial weight: it is the franchise's
  differentiator.
- Props that the story codes as non-weapons are never weaponised.

## Where the money actually is

Ranked by commercial return per unit of effort, for this cast:

1. **Silhouette recognisability at 64px.** Drives icons, stickers, keychains,
   acrylic stands and store thumbnails. Cheapest and highest-return lever.
2. **A memory point people can name.** Drives word of mouth, fan art and
   search. A detail someone can name in six words is a marketing asset.
3. **Expression range.** Drives emotional attachment across a long VN, and
   supplies sticker packs and reaction images essentially for free.
4. **Hero shot quality.** Drives the roll/skip and wishlist decision. One
   excellent hero shot per character beats five mediocre alternate costumes.
5. **Costume alternates.** Real revenue, but only *after* 1-4 are solid.

Note the ordering: the top three are all achievable at any age rating, apply to
the entire cast, and are where this project should spend its effort first.
