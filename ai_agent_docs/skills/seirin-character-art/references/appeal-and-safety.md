# Appeal and safety

How "commercially sellable" is achieved for each character, and the limits that
are not negotiable. This exists because "gacha appeal" and "cast containing
minors" have to be reconciled *at design time* — not argued about per asset.

## The commercial reality

Two facts hold at once:

1. Character appeal drives revenue. A roster that does not make people want the
   characters does not sell, no matter how good the writing is.
2. Sexualised depiction of minor characters gets a product **rejected** — by
   the App Store, Google Play, Steam, Nintendo, payment processors, ad networks
   and most publishers and platforms. A rejection or takedown after launch
   costs vastly more time and money than designing correctly the first time,
   and this project's own design canon (§11) already forbids it.

So the fastest route to release is: **maximum appeal, allocated by the mechanism
appropriate to each character.** Appeal is not one thing. Exposure is one narrow
lever, and it is the *least* differentiating one — it is the thing every
competitor already does. Silhouette, memory point and expression charisma are
what make a character screenshot-worthy and merchandisable, and they work for
every character in the cast.

## Appeal tracks

Each record carries an `appeal_track`.

### `hero_glamour` — adults

Reika (28), Saya (31), Kitsune (24), Lumina (35), Kurogane (48), Splash
(adult-coded non-human).

Full adult glamour. Mature proportions, confident posture, flattering
cinematic lighting, dynamic hero shots, tailored and characterful costume.
Sensuality is carried by **presence, authority, staging and competence** rather
than by exposure — which is also simply the stronger design choice: Lumina's
concealing floor-length robe and halo rig is more striking than a revealing
costume would be, and Kitsune's charisma lives in the deployed nine-tail rig
and the smirk, not in skin.

Their costumes are largely covering by design logic (Reika is in a pilot
harness, Saya in a lab coat, Kitsune in a technical bodysuit, Lumina in a ritual
column robe) because these are working people, and working clothes that fit the
job read as *more* attractive, not less. Adults may still be depicted with
mature figures and attractive framing — that is normal and expected.

### `cool_kid` — teens

Ren (17), Ryuki (16), Momo (15), Hana (13).

Appeal comes from **coolness, style and charisma**. These characters should be
the ones players want as an avatar or on a T-shirt.

Levers used at full strength:
- Boldest silhouettes in the roster (Momo's comet hem, Ren's knotted-sleeve
  delta, Ryuki's double head).
- Strongest memory points (the visor crown, the fibre-optic monitor, the
  heterochromia and thermochromic dress, the dragon hood).
- Expression charisma — Ren's smug sideways look, Momo's professional stage
  smile versus her flat off-camera face, Hana's rare genuine smile. Contrast
  between public and private faces is enormously appealing and costs nothing.
- Dynamic action staging, great costume design, effect layers.

Hard limits: full-coverage costumes (Momo's stage outfit is long-sleeved, high-
necked, with opaque tights — which is also accurate for a working idol);
age-accurate proportions and body types; no camera angles that emphasise the
body (no low upskirt angles, no chest-led framing, no over-the-shoulder rear
emphasis); no wardrobe malfunction, wet-clothing, or bath/swimsuit framing; no
romantic or sexual framing with adults.

### `mascot` — mascot-coded

Miya (5), Aster-7, Stella.

Appeal is **cuteness and merchandisability**. Maximum infant schema for Miya:
enormous round eyes with iris filling the opening, double highlights, 4.5-head
ratio, round cheeks, tiny hands. Her silhouette is deliberately the most
readable in the whole roster at thumbnail size because she is the merch
mascot — hat-plus-raven reads at 64px, which is exactly what a keychain, a
sticker pack and an app icon need.

Absolute limits for Miya: no sexualisation of any kind, in any framing, pose,
camera angle or costume — full stop; no adult proportions; no peril, gore or
distress imagery; no solo depiction in a hazardous location; no real weapon.

## Hard limits (all characters)

These are copied into every prompt's exclusion section:

- No nudity, underwear-as-costume, or sexualised framing of any character
  under 18 — Ren, Ryuki, Momo, Hana and Miya. No exceptions, no "it's just
  the pose", no alternate versions "for a different market".
- No romantic or sexual pairing of a minor with an adult character.
- No sexualised camera angles on minors: no low angle up the body, no framing
  led by chest or hips, no rear-emphasis.
- No gore, no self-harm imagery, no depiction of a minor in danger being
  presented as spectacle.
- Ryuki's ichthyosis is rendered as **fine pale plating, texture and dignity**
  — never as wounds, burns, scabs, gore or reptile scales, and never as an
  object of pity or horror.
- Reika's burn scar is never removed, minimised or "prettied". It is hers.
- No real magic or genuine supernatural effect anywhere in the franchise —
  every apparent miracle is staged, projected or technological (design canon
  §2.1). This is a *design* rule with commercial weight: it is the franchise's
  differentiator.
- No weaponisation of the safety-coded props: Momo's mic, Yuki's shinai,
  Kitsune's tails, Miya's Corvus, Aster-7's manipulators, Kurogane's prosthetic.

## Where the money actually is

Ranked by commercial return per unit of effort, for this cast:

1. **Silhouette recognisability at 64px.** Drives icons, stickers, keychains,
   acrylic stands and store thumbnails. Cheapest and highest-return lever.
2. **A memory point people can name.** Drives word of mouth, fan art and
   search. "The girl with the welding visor" is a marketing asset.
3. **Expression range.** Drives emotional attachment across a long VN, and
   supplies sticker packs and reaction images essentially for free.
4. **Hero shot quality.** Drives the roll/skip and wishlist decision. One
   excellent hero shot per character beats five mediocre alternate costumes.
5. **Costume alternates.** Real revenue, but only *after* 1-4 are solid.

Note the ordering: the top three are all achievable at any age rating, apply to
the entire cast, and are where this project should spend its effort first.
