# Wardrobe — questions and a catalogue to pull from

Clothing is where most anime and AI-generated character design quietly fails.
This file exists to make that failure visible and to widen the pool of answers.
It contains **questions and options**, not decisions.

## The three default failures

Name them so you can catch them.

1. **Sci-fi looks like a Catholic priest.** High collars, long dark robes,
   cassock silhouettes, sashes, a vestment-like layered coat. It is genuinely
   the most common AI sci-fi output. Ask: is this a designed choice, or the
   model's default? If the character *should* read as clerical, why?
2. **Everyone wears a bodysuit.** Smooth, seamless, form-fitting, no
   fastenings, no layers, no weight. It is not a garment — it is the absence of
   one. It has no construction, so nothing about it can tell you who wears it.
3. **Characters own one outfit.** Real people dress differently every day:
   for weather, for work, for who they are meeting, for what is clean. A
   character with a single costume is a mascot, not a person.

Fantasy-styled AI output frequently looks *better* than sci-fi output, and the
reason is instructive: fantasy costume is built from **real historical
garments** — layers, fastenings, fabric weight, visible construction. It has
things to look at. That is available to any genre.

## The core question

> **What would this person actually wear, given their job, their money, their
> city, today's weather — and where did those clothes come from?**

Everything else follows.

## Questions per character

### Ordinary day
- What are they wearing right now, on a normal working day?
- Does it suit the job the design document gives them?
- How did they get it — inherited, issued as a uniform, bought cheap, made,
  borrowed, stolen?
- Does it fit? Was it chosen by them, or by someone else?

### Weather and season
Seirin is a coastal city on Honshū: real rain, real winter, real humid summer.
- What do they wear when it rains? Coastal rain is constant in the design doc's
  own descriptions.
- What changes in winter? What in summer?
- Is there a layer they add and remove during a scene? (Layers that come off
  are enormously useful for VN sprite variants.)

### More than one outfit
- How many outfits do they own, and what are they?
- Which is the work one, the warm one, the good one, the sleeping one?
- What do they wear when they are *not* doing the thing they are known for?
- Which outfit shows a side of them the default outfit hides?

### Construction — the detail that makes cloth read as cloth
- How does it fasten? Buttons, ties, hooks, a belt, a zip, a wrap?
- How many layers, and which is on top?
- Is the fabric heavy or light? Does it hang, drape, stiffen, cling, swing?
- What is worn out, patched, stained, faded, too big, obviously repaired?
- What does the wear pattern say about the work they do?

### Provenance
- Which garment tradition does this draw on? (Catalogue below.)
- Why would *this* person, in *this* city, in this decade, plausibly own it?
- Is it worn correctly, or deliberately wrong — and if wrong, does the
  character know?

## Catalogue — traditions worth raiding

Nothing restricts a modern character to modern clothes. People wear what looks
good, what is warm, what they inherited, what their subculture wears, what
their religion or trade asks for. Take from anywhere, but **answer why**.

**Japanese** — kimono, yukata, haori, hakama, jinbei, samue, happi, hanten,
noragi work coats, jika-tabi, sashiko-stitched and boro-patched workwear,
kendo/kyūdō dress, shrine dress, festival matsuri wear, gakuran and sailor
uniforms, and the whole street-fashion lineage (Lolita, gyaru, mori, decora,
visual-kei).

**Working and functional** — mechanic coveralls, fishing oilskins, welding
leathers, dockworker gear, chef whites, hospital scrubs, hi-vis, hazmat and
clean-room suits, aircrew and diving suits, rail and postal uniforms. These
carry authority and history precisely because they are designed for a task.

**Historical and regional** — monastic and clerical dress (use *knowingly*),
academic robes, military dress across eras, hanfu, cheongsam, hanbok, ao dai,
sari, kurta, kaftan, djellaba, folk embroidery of Eastern Europe and the
Balkans, Andean textiles, West African wax print and boubou, Ainu and Okinawan
dress, Arctic parka construction, nomadic layered coats.

**Subcultural and contemporary** — punk, goth, techwear, workwear revival,
normcore, athleisure, hiking and outdoor kit, cycling gear, secondhand and
thrifted layering, uniforms worn wrong on purpose.

**Speculative — but built on something real** — near-future adaptations of any
of the above: a rain-shedding haori, a heated hanten, a coverall with a
service-history of patches, a shrine garment with a discreet sensor woven in.
Speculative clothing works when it is a *modification of a real garment* with a
visible reason, and fails when it is a smooth surface with glowing lines.

## Test before accepting an answer

- Could a costume department actually make this? From what, and how does it
  fasten?
- Would this person be cold, soaked or overheating in it?
- Does it read as *this character* in black silhouette?
- Is there anything to look at up close — seams, wear, layers, repair?
- Is it different from what every other cast member is wearing?
- Would a real person wear this two days running, and if not, what is day two?
- **Would you notice this outfit in a crowd of AI-generated anime characters?**
  If not, what is the reason to keep it?

## Standing rules

- The art agent does **not** invent wardrobe. It asks these questions and waits
  for answers, which go into `assets/cast.json`.
- "A bodysuit" and "sci-fi armour" are answers that require a stated reason.
- Every character needs at least two outfits before production. One is a
  costume; two is a person.
- If the answer sounds like every other anime character, that is the signal to
  ask another question, not to start drawing.
