# STORY BIBLE — *The Last Frequency*

> Living document. Written before and maintained during drafting, per the
> standard AI-assisted long-form workflow (bible + beat outline + per-scene
> recaps + continuity pass). If the draft solidifies something new, it lands
> here first. This story is **original** — it deliberately owes nothing to the
> repo's earlier "Seirin" design document or the Cyber-Nexus example script.

## 1. Logline

On the last night before their coastal radio station is shut down for good, a
young operator receives a distress call from a ship that sank forty-five years
ago — and the storm that took it is walking back in through the door tonight.

## 2. Genre / tone / audience

- Genre: grounded supernatural mystery, one night, one place. Science-fiction
  at the edges (a time fold), human drama at the core.
- Tone: quiet dread into warmth. Rain on glass, warm tube light, the hiss of a
  dead channel. Not horror. The uncanny is gentle and sad, never gory.
- Audience: all ages / 12+. No romance on screen beyond a hand briefly held; no
  violence beyond a storm and a burning antenna; no sexual content. The entire
  cast is adult (youngest is 26).
- Point of view: first person, the player character. Interior narration in
  present tense ("The console hums under my palms."). Dialogue lines in plain
  script form.
- House style rules (voice consistency pass enforces these):
  - Short declarative sentences for tension. Longer, rolling ones for memory.
  - The sea is never a metaphor machine — at most one poetic image per scene.
  - Each character gets distinct diction (see §4). No character ever says
    "indeed" except Harlan; no character ever swears except Jun ("damn", once).

## 3. Setting

**Meridian Point Relay Station** ("the Point"), on a basalt cape above the town
of Shiogara, northern coast. A maritime radio relay and atmospheric observatory
built in 1951, expanded after the *Cordelia* disaster of 1986 with the mandate:
"so that no call for help from this water is ever again unheard." By 2031 the
satellite network has made it redundant. The Maritime Authority schedules
decommissioning: final watch ends at dawn, 06:00, 18 October 2031. Remote
power-down of the transmitter is scheduled for 05:30.

Rooms (each = one background):
- **Watch room** — the heart. Curved console of dials and switches, two modern
  screens grafted onto a 1950s desk, the antique 1420 kHz tube receiver under
  glass ("the Old Set"), a rain-streaked window facing the sea.
- **Corridor** — dim green emergency lighting, numbered doors, linoleum.
- **Archive** — basement room: watch logs 1951–2031 on steel shelves, one
  working desk lamp, the smell of paper and salt.
- **Rooftop array** — the mast field in the storm: lattice masts, guy wires
  singing, the cape dropping away to black water.
- **Break room** — tiny kitchen: kettle, noodle cupboard, a table for four,
  warm yellow light. The emotional center of the station.
- **Cliff at dawn** — the cape walk outside, used for endings.
- **Title/exterior** — the station at night from the sea road: a low building,
  a red beacon, the mast field against storm cloud.

## 4. Cast (all adults; original characters)

**Noa Kiriya (player, default name Noa, 26)** — junior radio operator. Took the
final night shift because nobody else would. Quiet, dutiful, a good listener —
the kind of person people tell the truth to. First-person narrator; no sprite
(we see through their eyes). Want: to matter, once, before the lights go out.

**Mira Sayo (27)** — Maritime Authority decommissioning engineer, on site to
supervise the shutdown. Sprites: normal / smile / worried. Voice: clipped,
precise, dry teasing; engineering metaphors ("that's a load-bearing
assumption"); hides grief under competence. Secret: her grandmother **Chiyo
Sayo** was the *Cordelia*'s cook, lost at 29 in 1986. Mira was raised on
Chiyo's pre-voyage letters; she volunteered for this job to be present at the
station's end. Arc: from "I'm here to close a building" to "I'm here to answer
a call."

**Elara Vance (32)** — radio officer of the MV *Cordelia*, heard through the
fold. For her, tonight is the night of the sinking, 17–18 October 1986.
Sprites: normal / sad / hopeful (she is never shown on a ship — she appears as
a "signal made visible": the Old Set's dial glow resolving into her at the
watch-room mic; staging always keeps the dial light behind her). Voice: formal
1980s maritime register, wry under pressure, zero self-pity; calls Noa
"station" until Act 3, then by name. She has been keeping watch on a loop she
does not fully understand. Her line that closes Ending A is also the title's
meaning: someone must keep the last frequency warm.

**Elias Harlan (61)** — station chief, on his last day in the job he has held
for 22 years. Sprites: normal / stern. In 1986 he was the 22-year-old night
technician; he heard a fragment of the *Cordelia*'s call on the Old Set,
mistook it for storm noise, and logged only his initials and six words. Guilt
built his career. Voice: weary, paternal, careful; says "indeed"; finishes
other people's sentences with the correct word and then apologizes. Arc:
confession, then the choice to act this time.

**Jun Okita (34)** — night security guard, Shiogara local, fisherman's family.
Sprites: smile / alert. Makes midnight noodles for anyone awake after 01:00 —
his late mother's recipe; knows every family the 1986 memorial names. Voice:
easy, warm, plain-spoken; one "damn" allowed (Act 3, at the window); puns
about radio that are deliberately terrible. Function: the story's warmth and
its moral in plain words.

**Referenced, not seen:** Captain Ilsa Maro and the 13 others aboard the
*Cordelia*; Chiyo Sayo; the Authority voice on the phone (Act 3, Harlan's
scene only — no sprite, one line).

## 5. Rules of the anomaly (locked — do not bend in draft)

1. The fold opens inside the resonance storm (a ~40–45-year electromagnetic
   pattern: solar weather meeting the cape's magnetite ground). It opened the
   night the *Cordelia* sank (1986) and reopens tonight (2031). The town calls
   it "the night the sea repeats itself" — folklore with a real core.
2. Window: **02:33 → 05:07**. The *Cordelia* struck the reef at 04:44 in 1986.
3. Only the **Old Set** (the 1951 tube receiver/transmitter on 1420 kHz)
   crosses the fold. Modern gear reads it as structured interference.
4. The crossing is **two-way while the array is powered**. Words can go back.
   What is changed inside the fold rewrites the past.
5. **Memory rule:** people physically inside the station during the rewrite
   keep both memories. Everyone else wakes inside the new history as though it
   had always been true. (This is why Ending A leaves exactly Harlan and Noa
   remembering — Harlan was in the building in 1986 AND tonight; the rule is
   "inside the station, inside the fold," and Harlan insists on standing in the
   watch room for the broadcast.)
6. The fold cannot be held past 05:07 by will; it can only be *forced*, which
   burns the array (Ending C's price) and tears the channel mid-sentence.
7. The distress loop Elara sends is the same transmission in both years — she
   has been inside the fold's pocket the whole time; being *answered* is what
   lets her perceive a way out. Answering is the whole game's thesis: signals
   only save people if someone is listening, and listening is a decision.

## 6. Stats, flags, gates

- `clarity` (0–2): +1 from the Act 1 investigation (archive OR rooftop); +1
  from the Act 2 choice (share the log entry with Mira, OR stay silent but let
  Mira's telemetry confirm the fold — both paths can reach 2, via different
  lines).
- `trust` (0–3): +1 for answering the signal at first contact (Act 1 choice);
  +1 for hearing Mira's confession in the break room (automatic if reached);
  +1 for the Act 2 small choice (read Elara the crew manifest aloud / promise
  to remember the names).
- Flags: `answered_signal`, `went_archive` | `went_rooftop`, `shared_log`,
  `mira_confided`, `names_read`, `harlan_confessed`.
- **Ending gate (Act 3 big choice, option A "broadcast the bearing"):**
  requires `answered_signal` AND `clarity >= 2` AND `trust >= 2`. If chosen
  without the gate, `vn.branch` routes to the *weak-signal* variant: the
  bearing goes out but the fold is too thin — the *Cordelia* hears only static
  — and the story resolves into Ending B with its own honest lines about
  nearly being heard. No path is unwinnable; the gate changes the ending, not
  access to play.
- Options B (record and preserve) and C (overload the array) are always
  available.

## 7. Beat outline (scene = label; target words in parens)

- `Start` (1,150) — 22:00–01:15. Arrival at the Point; meet Jun; the mood of a
  place in its last hours; name input; the storm's leading edge; the 02:33
  signal breaks through on the Old Set. Choice 1: answer on air / log it as
  interference.
- `Act1_Answer` (750) / `Act1_Log` (700) — first contact variants. Elara's
  voice; the impossible date stamp. Wake Mira.
- `Act1_Together` (600) — Mira hears it; "interference doesn't breathe." She
  pulls the storm telemetry; the resonance pattern matches 1986. Choice 2:
  archive / rooftop.
- `Act2_Archive` (1,050) — the 1986 watch log; six words in young Harlan's
  hand; the *Cordelia*'s last known heading; clarity +1.
- `Act2_Rooftop` (1,050) — boosting the array in the storm with Mira; the
  signal clarifies; a lighthouse that should not exist on the water;
  clarity +1.
- `Act2_BreakRoom` (1,150) — Jun's noodles; Mira's confession about Chiyo;
  trust +1; Elara's long transmission: the ship is taking on water slowly,
  and from their window the crew can see a second storm — ours — with lights
  inside it. Choice 3: read her the crew manifest / promise to remember.
- `Act3_Harlan` (1,200) — 04:20. Harlan arrives in the storm; confrontation;
  the 1986 truth; the fold explained and confirmed; the 05:07 window and the
  05:30 remote power-down. Choice 4 (the big one): broadcast the bearing /
  record and preserve / overload the array.
- `Act3_BroadcastGate` (~120) — vn.branch on the gate.
- `Ending_HarborLight` (1,250) — A. The bearing goes out; the *Cordelia*
  turns; the rewrite; dawn with a living plaque; the morning phone call;
  Elara's last transmission (voice). Harlan and Noa remember both histories.
- `Ending_WeakSignal` (450) — A-failed-into-B variant; flows into B's dawn.
- `Ending_TheLongMemorial` (1,050) — B. The recording saved; the heritage
  reprieve; the names read aloud by the town; "they were heard."
- `Ending_Static` (1,150) — C. The array burns; the channel tears; 7 of 14
  saved including Chiyo; Elara stayed at the mic; one year later, a faint
  reply on 1420 kHz.
- **Script total target: ≥ 11,500 words** of dialogue + narration (verified by
  `tests/story.test.mjs`, which refuses to pass under 10,000).

## 9. Continuity checks (run before shipping)

- [ ] Clock monotonic per path: 22:00 → … → 05:07/06:00; HUD label per scene.
- [ ] Every label is reachable; every `jump` target exists (tested).
- [ ] No character says another character's signature words ("indeed",
  "load-bearing", the puns).
- [ ] 1986 math: Chiyo 29 then → born 1957; Mira's grandmother ✓. Harlan 22
  then → 61 now ✓ (2031 − 1986 = 45 years; "forty-five" everywhere, never
  "forty").
- [ ] Crew count: 14 aboard; Ending C saves 7; memorial lists 14 names in B,
  7 names in C's dawn.
- [ ] The Old Set is the only device that crosses the fold — modern screens
  never receive Elara directly.
- [ ] Word count ≥ 10,000 (target 11,500), machine-checked.
