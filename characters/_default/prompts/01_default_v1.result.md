# Default-idiom test v1 — Ren, Kitsune

**Verdict: PARTIAL — the direction is right, one correction needed.**

## What the new idiom won (keep all of it)
- **Contrast is transformed.** Deep near-black shadow with blazing highlights;
  the wide-value-range instruction works and should stay in every prompt.
- **Garment construction finally reads.** Ren's coverall is visibly an adult
  man's, cut down: raw unhemmed edge, oil staining at the thighs, mismatched
  patched knees, and the two empty sleeves knotted at the stomach. Six sessions
  of trying and this is the first time it is unambiguous.
- **Boots are no longer generic** — the single biggest per-item gain. Scuffed
  steel toe caps, caked lugged soles, mismatched cord laces, one strap undone.
  Calling footwear out as its own sentence fixed it.
- **Body shape reads as specific.** Ren is small, wiry and actually strong,
  bracing stance and all, instead of a default slim figure.
- **Kitsune's rig is completely legible as equipment**: padded hip yoke, black
  battery pack, webbing, cables running along nine booms. The 2.1 reconciliation
  is now self-evident without a word of explanation.
- **Cutting boilerplate cost nothing.** No text, watermarks, logos or extra
  fingers appeared in either image despite none being forbidden. Confirms the
  owner's point: those exclusions were dead weight.

## The one problem
**Removing ALL style language defaults the model to photorealism.** Ren is a
photograph of an actress; Kitsune is a photo of a cosplayer at a festival.
Both are good photographs and neither is the product — the project needs anime
sprites, and `questionnaire A.5` requires 2D flatness for transmedia use.

This is not an argument for the old style essays. The v1 style blocks were long,
self-contradictory and produced the 2.5D look. The fix is a **short anime
anchor** — a single clause naming the medium — while keeping every gain above
and none of the render-parameter micromanagement that caused 2.5D.

## Change for v2 (one change)
Add one short clause naming the medium and nothing else:
"Anime illustration, clean linework and cel colour" — no tone counts, no
gradient/rim-light instructions, no flatness lectures. Everything else in the
prompt stays exactly as it is.
